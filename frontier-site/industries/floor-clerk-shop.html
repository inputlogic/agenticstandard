The Other Side of the Counter

What the shop saw.

You just experienced the front of an agent. This is the back: what it learned, what it leaned on, and the fence it never crossed. Every retailer worries a live agent will say something weird to a customer. This is how you make that a governed system instead of a hope.

What it learned about you
  • Everything it knows, you said to its face: no bought data, no tracking pixels, no profile stitched from elsewhere.
What it refused to do
  • No fake urgency. It will never say “only 2 left” unless 2 is true.
  • No invented discounts. Prices come from the shelf, not the negotiation.
  • No checkout. Your card never touches the model. The store’s register does the money.
  • No follow-up email it decided to send. Nurture belongs to the Matchmaker, and a human approves those.
The fence, written down
  • Scope: greet, ask, recommend, build carts, draft gift notes. Nothing else.
  • Owner: the shop’s e-commerce lead, one named human.
  • Budget: capped per conversation, enforced in the request path. This one cost $0.03.
  • Review: first Friday, on carts built and customers helped, like any hire.
  • Trail: the full transcript, and every recommendation with its why.
The basket map it was walking

4,800 baskets, drawn as a map. The Basket Reader rebuilds it every night from the order history; the Floor Clerk walks it live, in conversation. The accent edges are the two it used on you. Market basket analysis is not a report in this shop. It is the sales floor.
