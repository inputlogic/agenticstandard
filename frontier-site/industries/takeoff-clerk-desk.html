Live Demo · Agent 02 · Field Services & Trades

The agent does the arithmetic. The assumptions stay yours.

You are the estimator at Ridgepole Site Services: fence, sanitation, bins, trench protection, everything a construction site needs before the first trade shows up. Quoting a full site is the hardest desk job in the shop. A teardown has one set of rules, a trench has another, the toilet count comes off a table in the safety reg, and the hoarding question is buried in a municipal bylaw. A good estimator holds all of it. Ridgepole's did, for thirty years, and Rob retired in March.

The agent on this desk is the Takeoff Clerk. A takeoff is the old estimating word for the count: what the job needs, counted before it is priced. The clerk reads the customer's messy email, pulls the lot off the parcel register, walks the rulebook, and builds the sheet line by line with the rule pinned to every number. What it will not do is guess silently or send. Three of the numbers on this sheet are assumptions, and the send button stays dim until each one is yours.

Under the hood

One loop. The same one on every desk we deploy.

The loop it runs
Trigger
the quote mailbox, and nothing else. A request lands, the clerk wakes. Between requests it watches, which costs about a tenth of a cent an hour.
Read
the rate card, the safety reg digest, the bylaw digest for the job's town, the parcel register, and two years of won jobs. Read-only, every one.
Think
rules first: the toilet table, the trench thresholds, the perimeter arithmetic and the takeoff factors are lookups that cost nothing. The model wakes only to read Mike's sentences and label what is stated, assumed and missing.
Act
a draft sheet with every line cited, assumptions flagged in amber, and a drafted clarifying question when a fact is load-bearing. It cannot send either one.
Your call
every assumption, every exception to a code line, the sharpen, and the send. The judgment on the sheet is a person's. The clerk just makes it legible.
Log
every rule that fired, every rate line read, every assumption and who disposed it, every cent of model time. When a customer disputes a line in August, the sheet answers for itself.

The fence around it · why this is safe to own
  • It reads the rate card. It cannot edit a rate, and a price under your discretion floor escalates to the owner instead of shipping.
  • It cites the reg and the bylaw. It cannot waive them silently. Strike a code line and the exclusion prints on the quote face with the citation attached.
  • Every assumption is labelled amber and listed. The send button is not wired until a person has disposed each one.
  • It drafts the email and the clarifying questions. The send button is wired to you. Nothing leaves the shop under its own name.
  • It reads your documents, not the open web. New reg and bylaw text lands through a monthly digest a person approves.
  • Every quote carries its trail: rules fired, rates read, assumptions and their owners, model spend. A disputed line answers for itself.
