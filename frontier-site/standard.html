The Beliefs

The Agentic Standard.

What we believe about AI agents at work, and the standard we hold ourselves to.
Why this exists

The tools arrive. The way you work is the missing piece.

We've spent our careers on every side of enterprise AI: building it, selling it, and buying it. We've watched well-funded tools fade away because nobody's daily work ever changed.

It happens everywhere, and it's nobody's fault. The tech arrives; the company doesn't change. Making that change is a whole job, and until now nobody made it theirs.

We made it ours. We put AI to work, then teach your team to run it without us.

This page is what we believe and the standard we hold ourselves to.

Seven Tenets

The beliefs.

Each one is a lesson we learned the hard way. These are the rules we're judged by.

01
On the gap

The gap is human.

Every failed AI rollout we've seen failed the same way. The model worked, the plan made sense, and the budget was there. What never changed: the daily work, and who owned the new thing on Monday morning.

The tech keeps getting better on its own. The bridge between the tech and your team's daily work is the product. That's the work we do.

02
On the work

Ship before you strategize.

We deploy working agents by week three. So the plan you get at week six rests on five weeks of work inside your company. Most firms give you a week of interviews.

Advice without proof is fiction. When we say a workflow is worth automating, it's because we watched it happen, talked to the person doing it, and built an agent that proves it.

03
On agents

Hire agents like employees.

Think "we hired someone." An agent needs a job description, a boss, a record of what it did, and regular reviews.

Treat agents like features and you get drift and surprises. Treat them like employees and the output compounds. The framing decides the outcome.

04
On governance

Rules are the accelerator.

Rules sound like friction. They're the opposite. Clear limits are what let agents act without asking permission every time.

The fastest teams we've seen have the clearest fences: agents know exactly what they may and may not do. Build the fence first. Speed comes from clarity.

05
On outcomes

The scoreboard is new capacity.

"Hours saved" is the wrong scoreboard. The number that matters is what your team can do now that it couldn't before.

Can a two-person marketing team work like five? Can leadership get the weekly update without a Sunday-night scramble? That's the measure.

06
On dependency

You should be able to fire us.

Every engagement ends with one question: could you run this without us? If the answer is no, we haven't done our job.

We leave clear docs, working systems, and people who know your AI setup inside out. The retainer is a choice you renew each time, and you can stop renewing whenever you like.

07
On the firm

We're builders.

We're engineers and operators. We've built production AI, shipped data platforms, and coached teams through the change.

If you want a 200-page strategy deck, bigger firms do that, and we'll point you to them. If you want AI working inside your company in six weeks, that's what we do. That's all we do.

The Standard

What you are owed.

This is the standard we hold ourselves to in every engagement.

If we miss, we say so and we fix it. No papering over.

When we live up to them, we get paid. When we don't, we don't. That's the Agentic Standard.
